6 subtle habits of people who handle high stress without burning out

It is true that there are many things in everyone's everyday life that invite stress. However, what matters is how people handle stress. Two people can face the exact same pressure and stress in life, but their approach to handling stress is what makes all the difference. Some people feel weighed down under stress and others emerge stronger from it.

The secret isn't a life free of chaos and stress. It lies in rewiring your brain in dealing with it. Moneycontrol team spoke to Dr Munia Bhattacharya, Senior Consultant of Clinical Psychology at Marengo Asia Hospitals, to undertand what are some of the habits of people who are able to handle high stress without feeling burnt out. "One thing I often tell people is that stress isn't really the villain. In fact, a certain amount of stress keeps us motivated. It helps us prepare for exams, meet deadlines, care for our families and solve problems. The issue begins when stress stops being temporary and quietly becomes normalised," she said.

"I've met people who have demanding jobs, ageing parents, young children and endless responsibilities, but they still look emotionally steady. Then I've also met people in India as well as overseas with comparatively less stressors in life, but who who feel completely overwhelmed. The difference is rarely the amount of stress. It's how one chooses to respond to it," she told Moneycontrol.

When we don't learn to manage stress, our body doesn't know when to switch off. We sleep poorly, become irritable, lose patience, make impulsive decisions and slowly stop enjoying things that once made us happy. Relationships begin to suffer because we start reacting instead of responding. Handling stress isn't about becoming emotionally tough, or pretending everything is okay. It's about giving your mind enough space to recover so that difficult days don't become a difficult life.

6 signs someone knows how to effectively handle stress
They don't rush to react
"One thing I've noticed in emotionally resilient people is that they don't answer every message immediately, they don't argue the moment they're angry and they don't make big decisions in the heat of emotion," Dr Bhattacharya said.

They've developed the habit of slowing themselves down. Sometimes it's just a few deep breaths, sometimes it's sleeping over a problem before responding. That small pause saves them from saying things they'll later regret.

They don't wear exhaustion like a badge of honour
Some people almost take pride in saying, "I haven't slept," or "I've been working non-stop." The people who cope well think differently. They know rest is part of being productive. They understand that a tired brain doesn't make good decisions. So they don't wait until they're completely drained before taking a break. That's not laziness. That's emotional intelligence.
They protect small routines even when life gets messy
When life becomes stressful, many people abandon the very habits that keep them mentally healthy. The people who stay emotionally balanced usually continue doing small things consistently. They try to sleep at a reasonable time, eat properly, go for a walk or spend a few quiet minutes with themselves. These habits may look normal on the outside, but psychologically they give stability when everything else is shaky.
They are comfortable seeking help
"A common misconception is that mentally strong people do everything on their own. In fact, I have found the opposite to be the case," Dr Bhattacharya added. Those who cope well with stress know when something is too heavy for one person to carry alone. They seek out a friend, family member or a professional before things get out of hand. They don’t see asking for help as a weakness. They see it as wisdom.
They stop trying to control everything
Stress often grows when we keep fighting things we simply cannot change. Emotionally resilient people don't waste all their energy asking, "Why is this happening?". Instead, they ask themselves, "What's the next best thing I can do?". That shift in thinking immediately reduces helplessness and gives them a sense of direction.

They recover quickly after setbacks
This doesn't mean they never feel hurt. They absolutely do. They feel disappointed, frustrated and anxious just like everyone else. The difference is that they don't build a permanent home around those emotions. They allow themselves to feel what they're feeling, learn whatever the experience has to teach them and then gradually move forward. That's resilience. Not the absence of pain, but the ability to return to yourself after difficult moments.

"When I look at people who seem calm under pressure, I rarely find those guys live easy lives. I find people who have quietly learnt one important skill , they know when to slow down, when to let go and when to begin again. That's what protects them from burnout. Resilience isn't built during life's biggest crises. It's built in the small choices we make every single day," Dr Bhattacharya told Moneycontrol.
