Lumio Aura 5 review

Simple. That word has long been missing from modern speakers. Somewhere along the way, home audio turned into a maze of companion apps, Wi-Fi pairing screens, firmware updates and subwoofers that need their own corner of the room. Almost all of us wanted good sound from the TV, the PC and the phone and a little big of convenience. 

The Lumio Aura 5 takes a step back, but only to move forward, like a tiger crouching low and drawing back before it pounces. That’s exactly how the Aura 5 feels, as if it were designed by Lumio. 

Priced at Rs 22,999, it is a pair of powered bookshelf speakers with a built-in amplifier, so there’s no separate amp box, no app to set up and no clutter of speaker wire. The name promises an “aura”, and no, we don’t mean the one people claim to sense in a room. Plug in a cable, pick a source, turn a knob, and you’re done. Even the feature list is easy to follow: 100W RMS, HDMI ARC, optical, coaxial, RCA, USB-A and Bluetooth 5.4.

And that is where things get interesting. Is keeping things simple still enough to handle gaming, a Marvel blockbuster and a Bluetooth playlist equally well? We used it for weeks with a PC, a TV and a smartphone to find out.

Lumio Aura 5: Design and build

The Aura 5 is a speaker that wants to be seen. Each composite-wood cabinet has a curved front panel, and the drivers are out in the open, with no fabric or mesh grille to hide them. The aluminium woofers and silk dome tweeters give the pair a studio-monitor look that suits a desk setup or a TV console with some style.

The look brings back the 90s nostalgia where a DVD or Blu-ray player used to come with their own two set of speakers. It looks retro and original by design. 

The open design has a downside, though. With nothing covering the cones, homes with curious kids or pets will need to think about where the speakers go. But Lumio has also added all the vents towards the front, which makes it easier to stack in a cabinet. But keep in mind that the cabinets aren’t small either, so measure your shelf or TV unit before you buy. 



The right speaker is the active unit and carries physical knobs for bass, treble and volume. Adjusting bass with a real knob while a movie is playing is much nicer than digging through menus. The two cabinets are linked by a zip cable to good old red and black wire – same as older speakers. So you can spread them apart for a proper stereo image, as long as you plan the cable route; that’s a flexibility we have been missing from modern speakers.

The only modern part it has is a really good-looking yet compact remote with an OLED display at the top. It shows the input source, volume level and sound mode, which helps in a dark room. It handles power, playback, volume, source, Bluetooth and EQ. I feel that the remote could  be a little more responsive, as there’s some latency I witnessed there. 

Lumio Aura 5: Connectivity and setup

This is where the Aura 5 earns much of its price. We tested it over HDMI ARC and with my PC over an AUX wired connection, and over Bluetooth with the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8. Optical, coaxial and USB-A are there too, so older set-top boxes, consoles and turntable preamps are all covered. The good thing is that it can also be connected via USB-A to USB-A to a PC and I tried that too and it works and delivers higher-quality audio output.



HDMI ARC with CEC works as expected. The TV remote controls the speaker volume, and the system powers on with the TV. Bluetooth 5.4 pairing with the Galaxy Z Fold8 took seconds, and the connection stayed stable around the room and in different rooms in a 1800 sq ft area. 

Lumio Aura 5: Gaming performance

I know that this speaker is designed to be an all-in-one companion with more focus on the TV viewing experience. But I feel that gaming is where the stereo separation really shows. In Capcom’s Pragmata, Hugh and the android Diana move through a lunar facility overrun by IDUS-controlled bots, and combat has you shooting with one hand and hacking with the other. With the cabinets about a metre apart on our desk, gunfire and enemy movement came clearly from the left or right, and we often turned towards a bot before it appeared on screen. The quieter Shelter scenes between Hugh and Diana were just as good, with Diana’s voice locked to the centre and sounding natural rather than boxy.

Phantom Blade Zero pushed things further. S-Game’s kung fu action game is built on fast blade exchanges, parries and long, multi-phase boss fights. In the battle against the Chief Disciple of the Seven Stars, the clang of steel and the whoosh of dodges stayed clean even at high volume, and the score building into the second phase never drowned out the combat. At its rated 105dB SPL, the Aura 5 gets very loud, and we could crank it up without distortion.



What I felt right is the good old classic bass output. Well, it may not be as deep and vibration-rich as the dedicated units, but it is indeed well pronounced and clean to its core. This is in fact the best I’ve heard in a stereo speaker in a while. 

Lumio Aura 5: Movie and TV experience

We watched Spider-Man: Brand New Day. Right away, the dialogue was the standout. Studio mode lifts voices, and the conversations between Peter and MJ, including their quieter moments, came through clearly without riding the volume.

The action holds up well. The prison brawl against The Hand, one of the film’s biggest set pieces, was a good stress test, with punches, impacts and background chaos layered across the stereo field. The web-swinging sequences over New York had a real sense of width, with Spider-Man’s movement sweeping from one cabinet to the other. Scenes with Jon Bernthal’s Punisher, full of heavier gunfire, could use more low-end weight, but it feels well balanced and if you are not too picky, then it feels alright and, again, clean and crisp too. 

Lumio Aura 5: Music performance

For music, we streamed over Bluetooth from the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 and mostly stayed in Studio mode. The live acoustic version of the Eagles’ Hotel California showed the stereo imaging best, with the guitars clearly separated between channels and the percussion sitting cleanly between them. Queen’s Bohemian Rhapsody, with its layered vocal harmonies, came across as open and well spread instead of crowded.

Indian music worked just as well. A.R. Rahman’s Kun Faya Kun let the vocals take centre stage with good warmth, and the silk dome tweeters kept the high end smooth rather than harsh at higher volumes. Podcasts were clear and easy to listen to for long stretches.

Groove mode stresses beats and rhythm and suited Daft Punk’s Get Lucky, giving the bassline and drums more energy. Bass-heavy tracks like Billie Eilish’s Bad Guy, though, showed the woofers’ limits. The bass was tight and controlled but lacked the deep rumble that bass lovers will want, despite the claimed 35Hz floor.

Lumio Aura 5: Verdict

The Lumio Aura 5 shows that simple can still be enough. At Rs 22,999, it is a pair of powered bookshelf speakers that skip the apps and the setup screens and focus on sound. Its retro look takes you back to the days of DVD players with their own speaker pairs, while the front-facing vents make it easy to fit into a TV cabinet or shelf. The classic red and black speaker wire lets you place the cabinets wherever the stereo image sounds best.

The sound is what makes the case for it. Stereo separation is excellent, whether you’re tracking IDUS bots in Pragmata, catching every blade clash in Phantom Blade Zero or following Spider-Man across New York in Brand New Day. Vocals stay clear and centred in film dialogue, podcasts and music alike, and the bass is punchy and clean. It won’t rattle the room like a dedicated subwoofer, but it is among the most balanced low-end we’ve heard from a stereo pair lately. Connectivity is just as complete, with HDMI ARC, optical, coaxial, RCA, USB-A for higher-quality PC audio, and a Bluetooth 5.4 connection that held steady across a 1,800 sq ft home.

There are trade-offs. The exposed drivers need careful placement in homes with kids or pets, the cabinets take up real space, and the remote’s slight lag takes some shine off an otherwise polished experience. Buyers chasing deep, room-shaking bass or true surround sound should look at a multi-channel home theatre system instead.

For everyone else who wants a clean sound with balanced bass and one setup for TV, gaming and music without the usual complications, the Lumio Aura 5 is easy to recommend.

Pros

Excellent left-right stereo separation
Clear, centred vocals and dialogue
Punchy, clean and well-balanced bass
Wide connectivity, including HDMI ARC, optical, coaxial, RCA, USB-A and Bluetooth 5.4
Retro design with front-facing vents for easy cabinet placement
Gets loud without distortion

Cons

Remote can be slow to respond
Exposed drivers with no grilles
